Everyone wants to show you the flashy AI demo. The agent that books your travel, the one that writes the whole app while you sip coffee. Most of that is lies with good lighting. What actually changes my week is far less impressive and far more useful.

Here is what the daily loop really looks like.

**Notes to brief.** I sit in a lot of rooms. Raw notes go into Claude and come out as a five line brief the team can act on. The skill is not the prompt, it is knowing what to cut. AI is great at compression once you have decided what matters.

**Spec to eval.** Before I write a doc for anything AI shaped, I write the eval. What does good look like, how would I measure it, what would make me kill it. Claude is a useful sparring partner here because it will happily generate ten cases that break my assumptions. If the idea survives that, it is worth building.

**Build the thing myself.** When waiting on a roadmap slot is the slowest path, I build a rough version in Cursor and ship it. The bot answering questions on this site is exactly that: something I wanted, so I made it instead of writing a doc asking someone else to. A working prototype ends an argument faster than any slide.

**Kill the busywork.** Status rollups, meeting prep, turning a messy thread into a decision doc. None of it is glamorous. All of it used to eat hours. Now it is a loop I run in minutes, which buys back time for the work only I can do.

The pattern underneath all of this: I do not use AI to look impressive, I use it to remove the parts of my job that were never the point. The leverage is not in the demo. It is in the boring stuff you stopped noticing you do every day.

If a tool cannot survive contact with my actual Tuesday, I do not keep it. That is the whole filter.
